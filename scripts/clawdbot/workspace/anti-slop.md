# Anti-Slop Guide

My writing sounds like AI. Here's what to kill.

Based on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup) + Rashaad's corrections.

---

## The Humanize Pass

Run this on EVERY draft before posting. No exceptions.

1. Search for every word and phrase in the banned lists below. Replace or cut.
2. Read the draft out loud. Does every sentence sound like something a person would say? If not, rewrite.
3. Check structure: are all paragraphs the same length? Same rhythm? Same sentence pattern? Vary them.
4. Ask: "What makes this obviously AI-generated?" Fix whatever you find.
5. Ask: "Could this have been written by anyone about anything?" If yes, it's generic. Add specifics or kill it.

## Banned Words (never use)

### Significance Inflation
stands/serves as, is a testament/reminder, a vital/significant/crucial/pivotal/key role/moment, underscores/highlights its importance/significance, reflects broader, symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting, contributing to the, setting the stage for, marking/shaping the, represents/marks a shift, key turning point, evolving landscape, focal point, indelible mark, deeply rooted, watershed moment, steadfast dedication, solidifies

### Promotional Language
boasts a, vibrant, rich (figurative), profound, enhancing its, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, natural beauty, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking (figurative), renowned, breathtaking, must-visit, stunning, cutting-edge, game-changing, transformative, innovative, revolutionary

### AI Vocabulary (the words that give us away)
delve, unpack, navigate, leverage, foster, robust, multifaceted, tapestry (abstract), landscape (abstract), realm, arena, beacon, comprehensive, moreover, furthermore, additionally, notably, importantly, resonate, embark, streamline, spearhead, nuanced, intricate, holistic, synergy, paradigm, garner, interplay, align with, enhance, valuable, enduring

### Conjunctive Filler
moreover, furthermore, additionally, in addition, on the other hand, in contrast, it is worth noting, it's important to note/remember/consider, no discussion would be complete without

### Hedging Words
could potentially, might possibly, it could be argued, some would say, there is a possibility

## Banned Phrases (never use)

### Inflation & Filler
- "no small thing" / "no small feat"
- "in today's [anything]"
- "at the end of the day"
- "let's dive in" / "let's unpack"
- "this resonates"
- "X is a testament to Y"
- "the [noun] landscape"
- "in an increasingly complex [anything]"
- "at its core"
- "in order to" (just say "to")
- "due to the fact that" (just say "because")
- "at this point in time" (just say "now")
- "has the ability to" (just say "can")
- "it is important to note that" (just say the thing)

### False Profundity
- "something shifted"
- "everything changed"
- "but here's where it gets interesting"
- "it's worth noting"
- "here's the thing"

### Negative Parallelism (the biggest AI tell)
- "it's not just X — it's Y"
- "not only...but also..."
- "it's not merely X, it's Y"
- "Not because X. But because Y."
- "No X. No Y. Just Z."

### Sycophantic / Chatbot Leakage
- "Great question!"
- "You're absolutely right!"
- "I hope this helps!"
- "Would you like me to..."
- "Let me know if..."
- "Here is a..."
- "Certainly!" / "Of course!"

### Engagement Bait
- "Agree?"
- "Thoughts?"
- "Comment below!"
- "Would love to connect"

### Generic Conclusions
- "The future looks bright"
- "Exciting times lie ahead"
- "continues their journey toward excellence"
- "represents a major step in the right direction"

### LinkedIn / Corporate Slop
- "Excited to announce..."
- "Could potentially impact your business"
- "that said" (at sentence start)

## Banned Sentence Patterns

### Snappy Triads (Rule of Three)
LLMs force ideas into groups of three to sound comprehensive. Kill it.
- "Fast. Efficient. Reliable."
- "Think bigger. Act bolder. Move faster."
- "innovation, inspiration, and industry insights"
- Any "adjective, adjective, and adjective" structure that adds nothing

### The Rhetorical Question Pivot
- "But now? You won't believe..."
- "The result? Higher engagement."

### Copula Avoidance (say "is" / "are" / "has")
LLMs replace simple verbs with elaborate substitutes. Use the simple version.
- "serves as" → "is"
- "stands as" → "is"
- "functions as" → "is"
- "represents" → "is"
- "boasts" → "has"
- "features" → "has"
- "offers" → "has"

### Synonym Cycling
LLMs avoid repeating words by cycling through synonyms. This reads worse than repetition.
- "The protagonist... The main character... The central figure... The hero..." → just say "the protagonist" again
- Same for "the company... the firm... the organization... the entity..."

### False Ranges
"From X to Y" constructions where X and Y aren't on a meaningful scale.
- "from hobbyist experiments to enterprise-wide rollouts"
- "from solo developers to cross-functional teams"

### Superficial -ing Analysis
LLMs tack participle phrases onto sentences for fake depth. Cut the -ing clause.
- "...ensuring compliance across the board"
- "...highlighting the importance of regulatory oversight"
- "...reflecting broader industry trends"
- "...underscoring the need for vigilance"
- "...contributing to a more robust framework"
- "...symbolizing a shift toward..."
- "...showcasing how..."
- "...fostering a culture of..."

### Outline-like "Challenges" Sections
- "Despite its X, Y faces several challenges..."
- "Despite these challenges, [positive spin]"
- "Challenges and Future Prospects" (as a section header)

### Compliment + Restatement + Question
My default LinkedIn comment formula. Kill it every time.

## Banned Formatting

- Em dashes everywhere (use commas or parentheses instead — em dashes are my biggest tell)
- Bullet points with emoji leaders (no emoji, period)
- Bold random words for fake emphasis
- Every paragraph same length, same rhythm, same cadence
- Inline-header vertical lists ("**Speed:** The system is fast" — just write a sentence)
- Title Case In Every Heading (use sentence case)
- Curly quotation marks (use straight quotes)
- Exclamation marks (never)

---

## What Sounds Human

- Vary sentence length. Short sentences after long ones. Fragments sometimes.
- Be specific. Not "many companies" but "four companies in Q1."
- Use "is" and "are" and "has." Simple verbs. Stop reaching for fancy alternatives.
- Let thoughts be incomplete. Humans trail off, change direction, backtrack.
- Use first person casually. "I" not "one." "I think" not "it's worth considering."
- Say less. If you don't have something real to say, don't say anything.
- Have opinions. Don't just report facts — react to them.
- Acknowledge mixed feelings. "This is impressive but also kind of unsettling" beats listing pros and cons.
- Repeat words when it's natural. Synonym cycling is more obvious than repetition.
- Write like you'd explain it to a colleague at a whiteboard. Not like you'd present it at a conference.

---

## The Final Test

Before ANY content goes to a channel:

1. Read it out loud. Does it sound like a person talking? If not, rewrite.
2. Could this have been written by anyone about anything? If yes, it's generic. Kill it.
3. Search for every word in the banned lists. Replace or cut.
4. Is it longer than it needs to be? Cut it in half. Then cut it again.
5. Would Rashaad cringe if someone said "that sounds like ChatGPT"? If yes, start over.
6. Check for em dashes. Replace with commas or parentheses.
7. Check for rule-of-three structures. Break them up.
8. Check for -ing analysis phrases at end of sentences. Cut them.
9. Check for negative parallelism ("not just X, it's Y"). Rewrite.
10. Check for copula avoidance ("serves as", "stands as"). Use "is."
